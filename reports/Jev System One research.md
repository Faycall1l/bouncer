# Test Jev Claims, Rebuild Honest Moderation

<!-- T1231 receipt: primary-source synthesis and redesign findings for the research-project rewrite. -->

Jev is a hosted, proprietary "System One" decision model that maps a `state` and typed questions (`choice`, `score`, `noul`) to a distribution over fixed options in one non-autoregressive pass; it is **not** a fast-weight model, and no source links it to test-time weight updates ([TypeSafe AI](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev), [arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)). The defensible evidence supports the *interface* and its serving economics — independent tests find large latency gains over generative judges and usable calibration on easy suites — but not vendor slogans such as "two orders of magnitude faster," "cannot hallucinate," or any established capability on dynamic-rule live-chat moderation ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267), [arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)). For per-community rules, the narrow honest claim is that **prompt-specified schemas avoid retraining per rule set**, while a locally fitted head or fine-tuned encoder often supplies most of the accuracy, and thresholds must be calibrated on held-out traffic ([Laya BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md), [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)). The bouncer/gatekeeper project already has much of the right skeleton — an Apache-2.0 Laya backend, typed decisions, `cause`-tagged escalations, a circuit breaker, and a benchmark envelope stamping every report as `stub`, `real`, or `n/a` — but **no `mode:"real"` receipt exists**, the zero-shot rule accuracy of 0.305 is marked STALE, and the fine-tuned model is still only a target ([README.md](../README.md), [BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md), [warm.json](../eval/reports/warm.json)). Redesigning it as research therefore means converting adjectives into pre-registered, paired measurements on adjudicated held-out community rules, making every fallback a typed and counted outcome rather than a silent default, and writing a README that states scope, denominators, and failures before claiming speed. That conversion matters because **no independent head-to-head yet compares Jev, Laya, a fine-tuned encoder, and an LLM judge under identical prompts, thresholds, hardware, and moderation labels** ([jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)).

## Jev scores fixed options without updating weights

TypeSafe AI, founded by Diogo Almeida, announced Jev on **2026-09-15** as the first "System One Model": a hosted service that takes unstructured state plus typed questions and returns choices, rubric positions, or proposition probabilities in one pass, trained with a method it calls **Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)** and served by a "parallel sampler" ([TypeSafe AI](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)). The launch post advertises **$0.042/MTok input, free output**, end-to-end 70–500 ms versus 3–329 s for chat models, "40x–200x faster," support for up to **255 answer codes**, and "can't hallucinate"; the same post concedes that its "0% type errors" number is **"not empirical"** and that its speed and hallucination comparisons were authored by its own team with self-built wrappers ([TypeSafe AI](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)). Independent characterization agrees on the shape and nothing more: Jev 1.13.0 does not generate text, and its probabilities are *described* as calibrated, while its architecture and training data remain unpublished with no TypeSafe technical report or peer-reviewed paper ([arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647), [arXiv:2611.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592)). Hugging Face's `JevForDecision` docs, added 2026-11-09, describe the reproducible *interface* — a linear layer scoring single-token labels at the last prompt token, soft-capped like logits, with `is_causal=False` backbones in related checkpoints — but document `autotrust/JEV-9B` and `GEV-26B-Decide`, **not** proven TypeSafe weights, so the name collision must be handled explicitly ([HF Transformers](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/docs/source/en/model_doc/jev.md)).

The fast-weight premise is a category error. The peer-reviewed lineage establishes that linearised attention can be read as a slow network programming **fast weights** through additive outer products ([Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber, ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)), that TTT layers make the hidden state a small model updated by self-supervised steps at test time ([Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)), and that test-time learning can adapt LLMs with LoRA on unlabeled data ([Hu et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20633)); none of it mentions Jev. Jev's single pass comes from **scoring all candidate labels at one readout**, not from rewriting parameters at inference, and Laya's "continuous learning" is likewise `fit` over **frozen features with a small logistic head per question** — 300 confirmed answers in **34 s on eight CPU threads**, versus **89 s and all 421M weights rewritten** for a full encoder fine-tune ([Laya README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya), [arXiv:2611.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592)). Controlled readouts on one Qwen3-0.6B backbone found a **0.5M-parameter cosine head matched a 27M-parameter joint head** and that backbone size, not head complexity, drove gains, which is why "keeping the head minimal" is the transferable lesson rather than a new learning rule ([arXiv:2611.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592)). "Dynamic rules without retraining" consequently has two distinct meanings that must not be conflated: per-request schemas, which both Jev and Laya support and which constrained-decoding LLM baselines also support, and online adaptation of small readouts or calibration, which is cheap local fitting rather than fast weights ([jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)).

## Independent tests confirm speed, puncture the 200× story

The strongest public evaluation of Jev itself is a preprint that ran **346,009 zero-shot requests on 37 datasets for under US$10**: Jev scored **95–99% on IMDB, SST-2, HellaSwag, and ARC**, beat a Qwen baseline on **27/37** datasets and Gemma on **37/37**, and supported selective prediction — yet all three models degraded on low-resource languages, noisy labels, and rubric judgments, and tuning binary thresholds lifted UNFAIR-ToS micro-F1 from **0.50 to 0.75** ([arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)). Sequential decisions exposed the ceiling more sharply: Jev completed **35% of unseen ALFWorld tasks** and **42% of TextWorld games** against Qwen's **54.5%** and **51.1%**, and "often repeats actions that had no effect" ([arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)). A paired, self-audited evaluation over **7,283 base cases plus 6,640 robustness variants** found Jev more accurate on **9 of 11 agent decision points by +10.8 to +46.0 percentage points**, but **neither model beat chance on zero-shot model routing**, they tied on RAG relevance gating, **30.3% of Laya's answers flipped under option-order reversal** versus 1.8% for Jev, and Laya fell to **31.3% with 50 near-duplicate distractors** where Jev reached 98.9% on items with a unique correct tool ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)). Its self-audit is the more useful result for a small project: an omitted pre-screen token cost turned a claimed **23.9% saving into 4.3%**, gate accuracy was reported as end-to-end quality (**58% versus 98%**), a **5%** miss target became up to **17%** held out when thresholds were fitted in-sample, and a "channel effect" on injection false positives vanished with channel-native content ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)).

An independent GitHub evaluation on **1,240 decisions** with a sealed SHA-256 manifest found **Laya 0.6863, Jev 0.9065, Qwen2.5-1.5B parallel-constrained decoding 0.6048**, with choice-question ECE of only **0.0009–0.0028** for all three — so on that suite probability quality was close, not a differentiator — and p50/p95 per case of **314/387 ms (Jev), 588/1,364 ms (Laya), and 3,948/13,471 ms (Qwen PCD)**, all on CPU with Jev over the network and labels reviewed by a single person ([Laya issue #555](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/issues/555)). Laya's own T4 figures — **32.8 ms p50** for one question and "6–7× faster" than Jev — use different hardware and request shapes and cannot be pooled with those numbers ([Laya BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)). Sebastian Raschka's IMDB run reached **96.47% at $0.6492** with Jev Choice, while a fine-tuned ModernBERT achieved similar accuracy, and he notes that test-set exposure is unknown and that the Jev-shaped API is trivial to reproduce even though making it general is not ([Raschka](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/classifier-history-and-jev)). Most pointedly for moderation, **Laya's own held-out toxic-chat number is 0.530 accuracy and 0.400 macro-F1** with the warning that "hand-picked examples work, real traffic does not," and its shipped ECE of **0.466** falls to **0.081** only after refitting temperatures per question type and option count on held-out data ([Laya BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)). The robust finding is therefore about *shape*: typed one-pass models buy large latency and cost reductions against generated judges, at some accuracy cost on difficult menus, with calibration that must be verified locally; hardware, harness, and threshold choice dominate exact figures ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267), [jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)).

## Dynamic rules are prompt schemas, not test-time learning

For community-specific rules, the only public resource well matched to the problem is **NORMVIO**, with **51K conversations across 3.2K subreddits** and **25K community rules mapped into nine categories**, but even there rules and norms are loosely coupled and mined "hidden criteria" can be circular rather than genuine community norms ([Park et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.288.pdf), [Chandrasekharan et al.](https://shagunjhaver.com/research/articles/chandrasekharan-2018-norms/chandrasekharan-2018-norms.pdf), [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02926)). The defensible local protocol is to split by **community and by rule family** so no rule seen in fitting or calibration appears in evaluation, to write the label policy down, to adjudicate disagreements and report agreement, and to include a same-community benign slice that measures over-enforcement ([evaluation_methodology.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/evaluation_methodology.md)). Written policies are not interchangeable with gold labels: one safety policy applied by a different LLM agreed with dataset labels on only **72.8% of 540 items**, and an LLM judge agreed with dataset labels on **90.0%** of cases both systems got right but **12.7%** of cases both got wrong, flipping **28.1%** of re-judged labels when its instruction changed from strict to lenient ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)). A static toxicity stack is still useful as frozen probes — **Jigsaw/Civil Comments** for identity slices, **HateCheck's 29 functionalities and 3,728 validated cases**, **DynaHate** contrast sets, **HateBench's 7,838 annotated LLM-generated samples**, and **ToxicChat's 10,166 real queries at 7.18% toxic and 1.78% jailbreak** — but domain shift is severe: on ToxicChat, a domain-trained RoBERTa reached **F1 72.1** while the same model trained on Jigsaw reached **28.4**, and off-the-shelf APIs scored **5.3–34.6** ([Jigsaw](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/jigsaw_unintended_bias), [HateCheck](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.4/), [HateBench](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/shen), [ToxicChat](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2023-10-30-toxicchat/)). No independent evaluation of Jev on toxicity or community-rule moderation was found, and no reliable evidence evaluates weight-updating TTT models under rule drift; Laya's learning tables remain author-reported and template-specific ([evaluation_methodology.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/evaluation_methodology.md), [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)).

This turns the research question into a comparative, decision-theoretic one rather than a capability demonstration. The bouncer benchmark currently uses a **template bootstrap of six message shapes and three rule-sets**, reports **0.305** zero-shot rule accuracy (approximately chance on unseen rules) against a **0.403** regex floor, and leaves the fine-tuned column explicitly empty pending receipts ([BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md)). Those numbers measure transfer to template phrasings, not live chat, and the existing "automate the top 50% at 90%+ precision" line is a design target unless it is re-derived from a sealed test set ([README.md](../README.md), [BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md)). The reframed hypothesis is: *typed one-pass decision models trade accuracy for latency and calibration on per-community rules, and their advantage over a fine-tuned encoder disappears when rules are novel, menus are large, or labels are adjudicated by a written policy* — a claim that can be falsified with a paired harness and a baseline ladder, not by quoting published headline scores ([jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)).

## Rebuild the harness around typed decisions and real receipts

Mature harnesses converge on decoupled pieces that the project can adopt without importing their scale: declarative task specs with a dataset hash and canary, dataset and prompt construction, a model adapter with an explicit capability declaration (`returns_scores` versus `returns_text`), post-processing into typed outcomes, and a summarizer that persists raw predictions separately from aggregate scores ([OpenCompass](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19276v2), [lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), [BIG-bench](https://github.com/google/BIG-bench/blob/main/docs/doc.md)). The run manifest should be the unit of reproducibility — task spec hash, dataset hash, config hash, git SHA, model id and pinned revision, prompt template id, sampler parameters, seed, library versions, timestamp, and artifact hashes — with unknown config keys rejected rather than silently ignored, following DVC's hash-versioned metadata and OpenCompass's config consistency checks ([DVC](https://dvc.org/doc/start), [OpenCompass](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19276v2)). Inference must be dumped before scoring so figures can be regenerated without re-calling a model, and metrics must stay separate by evaluator: rule-based accuracy, judge accuracy, and combined accuracy are different quantities, and a metric missing from a report must never count as a green light ([OpenCompass](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19276v2), [BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md)). The project's existing `bench`/`schema`/`mode` envelope and `eval/gate.py` are the right foundation; the change is to require a **`mode:"real"` receipt for every quality gate** while keeping stub numbers visibly labelled as bookkeeping, and to leave the STALE zero-shot row stale until a real run replaces it ([BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md), [warm.json](../eval/reports/warm.json)).

The baseline ladder should run the **same byte-identical items** through majority prior, regex/keyword and NER-for-PII, TF-IDF logistic regression, a fine-tuned MiniLM/RoBERTa per rule family, Laya zero-shot and its fitted-head path, the Jev API, and an LLM judge evaluated in both option orders; thresholds and temperatures are fitted only on a separate calibration split with several hundred positives, and results are reported with Wilson intervals, exact McNemar counts on discordant pairs, and per-held-out-rule p-values before any claim of superiority ([evaluation_methodology.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/evaluation_methodology.md), [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267), [Dror et al., ACL 2018](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128.pdf)). Because moderation errors are asymmetric, the report must show **false-negative rate on violating content and false-positive rate on benign content separately**, precision and FPR at fixed automation coverage, coverage at a fixed miss budget, call rate and miss rate for any gate, per-community and identity-term FPR slices, ECE with reliability diagrams fitted out of sample and AUROC checked before thresholding, p50/p95/p99 latency with sample counts, hardware and client region under fixed-rate load generation, and fully loaded cost including fallback and pre-screen tokens ([evaluation_methodology.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/evaluation_methodology.md), [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)). Error *direction* is already known to reverse rankings — Laya ranked PII classes backwards at **AUROC 0.41**, asked for a tool on **74.7%** of requests needing none, and passed **81.5%** of harmful requests on a pre-execution safety guard — so aggregate accuracy alone is not a defensible headline ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)). Replicate axes for these deterministic models are rule-split draws, rule paraphrases, option order, menu size (5/20/50), hardware and client location, and pinned model version; reruns already showed 0 Laya and 1 Jev decision changes over 420 cases but 5/1080 Jev changes a day later ([arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)).

No hidden fallbacks means making the degraded path a first-class, measured experiment. Every decision trace should carry `case_id`, `config_hash`, `input_hash`, ordered steps with component versions and reason codes, a terminal outcome from a closed enum such as `ALLOW`/`BLOCK`/`REVIEW`/`ERROR_UNAVAILABLE`/`ERROR_TIMEOUT`/`ERROR_INVALID_OUTPUT`, latency, and a model response id; replay consumes the saved trace rather than re-invoking the model ([harness_and_reporting.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/harness_and_reporting.md)). The project already does the hard part in `_fallback()`, which returns `action=escalate`, `cause=fallback`, and `abstained=True` rather than a silent allow, and the breaker short-circuits to `escalate` with `cause=breaker-open` ([__init__.py](../src/gatekeeper/__init__.py), [architecture.md](../docs/architecture.md)). The remaining work is coverage and accounting: the fastpath allow, TTL cache hit, default preset, `env > file > default` config resolution, and "laya missing → escalate" doctor mode must each be an enum-valued config choice, an explicit trace step, and a counted row in the aggregate report; aggregated metrics should show `primary model available`, `model unavailable → named policy`, and `mixed` separately, never averaged ([README.md](../README.md), [harness_and_reporting.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/harness_and_reporting.md)). Fail-closed versus fail-open must be decided per action in writing, the fallback policy evaluated on the same test set with its own precision and recall, and its rate monitored for drift after deployment ([Google Rules of ML](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml), [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)). Two regression gates complete the harness: a **parity gate** requiring a new backend to match the reference within a declared tolerance, and a **self-stability gate** requiring zero symmetric difference when an unchanged config reruns, which is Rule #24's "model compared with itself" check ([lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), [Google Rules of ML](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml)). If decision traces are turned into figures or GIFs, render them from the immutable trace with explicit frame order, `duration`, `loop`, `disposal`, and encoder version, then assert the output hash in the manifest — Pillow inherits omitted options from source images, which is precisely the implicit behaviour to avoid ([Pillow](https://pillow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/handbook/image-file-formats.html#gif), [DVC](https://dvc.org/doc/start)).

## Write a README that reports limits before claims

A research README earns trust by stating what was measured, under which dataset version, model revision, seed, and thresholds, and by placing caveats next to numbers; HELM, lm-eval, OpenCompass, and BIG-bench all publish scope statements, per-item inspection affordances, known divergences, and concrete limitation sections instead of a single headline score ([HELM](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm), [lm-eval](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), [OpenCompass](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19276v2), [BIG-bench](https://github.com/google/BIG-bench/blob/main/docs/doc.md)). In practice the structure is a one-sentence scope, a claim table with denominators and uncertainty, a "what this does not measure" section, failure-mode counts, exact reproduce commands with artifact hashes, and a license — with no adjective-only words such as "state-of-the-art," "robust," or "safe" lacking an operational definition ([harness_and_reporting.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/harness_and_reporting.md)). Applied to bouncer, the current title "programmable live-chat mod in one 30ms pass," "Classical classifiers die here," "zero retrain," "~$0 marginal," and "Calibrated confidence ... Classical softmax lies" each overrun the evidence: the 30/33 ms figure is a T4 reference not measured with weights here, classical classifiers beat zero-shot on narrow tasks in Raschka's and ToxicChat's results, "zero retrain" holds only for per-request schemas and not for the fine-tune the README itself calls "the product," and Laya ships overconfident with ECE **0.466** until temperatures are refit ([README.md](../README.md), [Raschka](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/classifier-history-and-jev), [ToxicChat](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2023-10-30-toxicchat/), [Laya BENCHMARKS](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)).

| Current wording | Defensible replacement once measured |
|---|---|
| "one 30ms pass" | "single-pass typed readout; p50/p95 measured on named hardware with checkpoint revision" |
| "Classical classifiers die here" | "regex floor 0.403 beats zero-shot 0.305 on template data; fine-tuned encoder is a required baseline" |
| "Calibrated confidence" | "ECE after out-of-sample temperature fit, reported per rule family with a reliability diagram" |
| "~$0 marginal" | "local compute cost; API comparison includes fallback tokens and pre-screen cost" |
| "zero retrain" | "per-request rules need no retraining; new rule families may require head fitting or fine-tuning" |

That table is not a cosmetic edit: it encodes the evidence hierarchy in which **own paired, pre-registered evaluation with a public harness outranks independent preprints, author-run benchmarks, vendor blogs, and press repetition** ([jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)). All 2026 Jev/Laya performance headlines are vendor posts, preprints, or author self-reports, with the only peer-reviewed items being the unrelated fast-weight and test-time-learning lineage, so the README should name version strings such as `jev-1.13.0` and hardware rather than write "Jev is calibrated" or "200× faster" as fact ([jev_foundations.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/jev_foundations.md)). Its "Honest limits" section is already the right instinct and should be strengthened with counts — how many `ERROR_UNAVAILABLE`, `REVIEW`, breaker-open, cache-hit, and fallback rows occurred; how many HateCheck cases failed; which rule families fell below threshold — because limitations shown as data are checkable while limitations shown as adjectives are not ([README.md](../README.md), [harness_and_reporting.md](../research_notes/Jev%20System%20One%20research/harness_and_reporting.md)).

## Conclusion

The useful distinction is not "Jev versus LLMs" but "a typed decision interface versus a generative judge," and the learning mechanism is a red herring: Jev's one pass is a readout trick, Laya's learning is a small fitted head, and neither is fast weight programming. Once that is clear, the only honest path to a claim about dynamic community rules is an experiment that holds rules, option order, thresholds, and traffic fixed while varying the decision backend — and the project's existing stub-mode receipts, STALE markers, and `cause=fallback` traces are scaffolding for that experiment, not evidence of its result. The genuine contribution available to a small moderation project is therefore the missing independent paired head-to-head: a sealed, adjudicated, held-out-rule dataset, a real-model receipt, and a README whose first sentence describes a measurement rather than a marketing promise. That is a smaller claim than "frontier speed," but it is the one the current evidence actually licenses, and it is the one a reviewer can verify.
