# Jev, System One decision models, fast weights, and Laya — foundations brief

Scope note: research current to **2026-11-24**. Source labels used throughout: **[VENDOR]** = company marketing/blog, **[SELF]** = author/repo self-report, **[PREPRINT]** = arXiv/Preprints.org (not peer-reviewed unless stated), **[PEER]** = conference/journal, **[IND]** = third-party evaluation, **[DOC]** = framework documentation, **[SEC]** = secondary press/blog. The single most important correction to the assignment premise: **Jev is not a fast-weight / test-time-training model in the technical sense. No source found links TypeSafe's Jev to fast weights.** The mechanisms are different: Jev/Laya are *non-autoregressive decision heads over an LLM/encoder backbone*; "learning" in Laya 0.5 is small fitted heads over frozen features plus conformal calibration, not in-weight fast updates.

## Key Question 1: What is Jev, who introduced it, and what problem does it claim to solve?

### Takeaway
Jev is a **hosted, proprietary "System One" decision model** announced by **TypeSafe AI** (founder **Diogo Almeida**, formerly of OpenAI/InstructGPT work) on **2026-09-15**. It takes a `state` plus typed questions (`choice`, `score`, `noul`) and returns a distribution over fixed options in one non-autoregressive pass; the claimed problem is that LLM chat/structured-output calls are too slow, expensive, uncalibrated, and parse-fragile for in-code decisions such as moderation, routing, scoring and guardrails. Its architecture and training data are **unpublished**, and there is no peer-reviewed paper by TypeSafe.

### Inferences
- The product category is well specified (typed, probabilistic, single-pass decisions), but the "new architecture" claim is not publicly substantiated; reproducing the *interface* is trivial, reproducing the *quality* is the hard part (Raschka makes exactly this point).
- The problem framing is an engineering/UX claim (in-code decisions, no parsing, latency, calibrated thresholds), not a claim of new general intelligence. For a moderation demo, the relevant hypothesis is "typed decision model + threshold/abstain" vs "LLM judge" vs "fine-tuned encoder", not "Jev vs GPT on reasoning".
- Name collision risk: `autotrust/JEV-9B` and `GEV-26B-Decide` are public HF checkpoints using the Jev decision head; do not cite them as TypeSafe Jev weights.

### Gaps
- No TypeSafe technical report, architecture diagram, parameter count, training-data description, or peer-reviewed paper for Jev was found. Vendor FAQ answers on internals were not rendered in the fetched page.
- No public Jev weights; hosted API only, early access at announcement; pricing long-term sustainability admitted as unproven by vendor.
- Independent verification of "trained exclusively on synthetic data" is missing (TechCrunch paywall/CF).

## Key Question 2: How do fast weights / test-time weight updates work, and does Jev use them?

### Takeaway
**No evidence was found that Jev uses fast weights or test-time weight updates.** Fast-weight/testing-time-training literature is a distinct 1990s→2021→2024→2025 lineage whose mechanism is *updating model parameters or a hidden-state model at inference*. Jev's single-pass property comes from **scoring all candidate labels in one prompt evaluation** (no decoding), not from weight adaptation. Laya's "keeps learning" is likewise fitting small heads/calibration on frozen features, not rewriting backbone weights.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER, 2021]** *Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers*, **Imanol Schlag, Kazuki Irie, Jürgen Schmidhuber**, arXiv:2102.11174 (v1 **2021-02-22**, v3 **2021-06-09**), ICML 2021: "formal equivalence of linearised self-attention mechanisms and fast weight controllers from the early '90s, where a 'slow' neural net learns by gradient descent to program the 'fast weights' of another net through sequences of elementary programming instructions which are additive outer products of self-invented activation patterns (today called keys and values)"; adds a delta-rule-like correction and learned dynamic learning rates. — [arXiv:2102.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)
- **[PREPRINT, 2024–2025]** *Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States*, **Yu Sun et al.**, arXiv:2407.04620 (v1 **2024-07-05**, v4 **2025-08-31**): "make the hidden state a machine learning model itself, and the update rule a step of self-supervised learning. Since the hidden state is updated by training even on test sequences, our layers are called **Test-Time Training (TTT) layers**"; TTT-Linear/TTT-MLP at 125M–1.3B; TTT-MLP "still faces challenges in memory I/O". — [arXiv:2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)
- **[PEER, 2025]** *Test-Time Learning for Large Language Models*, **Jinwu Hu, Zhitian Zhang, Guohao Chen, Xutao Wen, Chao Shuai, Wei Luo, Bin Xiao, Yuanqing Li, Mingkui Tan**, arXiv:2505.20633 (**2025-05-27**, **accepted ICML 2025**): TLM adapts LLMs to target domains using only unlabeled test data by "input perplexity minimization", emphasizes high-perplexity samples, and uses **LoRA instead of full-parameter optimization** to reduce forgetting; reports "at least 20%" improvement on AdaptEval domain adaptation. This is *test-time learning* but not Jev and not described as fast weights. — [arXiv:2505.20633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20633)
- **[PREPRINT, 2026]** Related TTT/fast-weight work found but not read in depth: *Modular TTT: Rethinking Test-Time Training as Composable Modules*, arXiv:2608.07110 ("fast weights are updated by an internal learning rule"); *In-Place Test-Time Training* (OpenReview, G. Feng et al., "how effectively the fast weights can store, retrieve, and forget sequential information"); NVIDIA TTT-KVB ("Each layer maintains fast weights fθ, typically a small MLP, updated during both training and inference", [research.nvidia.com](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/tttla/)). None mentions Jev or Laya in retrieved snippets. — [arXiv:2608.07110](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07110v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=dTWfCLSoyl)
- **[PREPRINT/IND]** Controlled ablation of Jev-*style* models: Rastogi re-implements five readouts on the same Qwen3-0.6B backbone and 15 public English training sources: all readouts reach **81.3–82.0%** in-domain; a **0.5M-parameter cosine head** matches a **27M-parameter joint schema head** at half the inference time; one prompt for all questions matches one-prompt-per-question accuracy with **1.6× faster inference**; backbone size matters more (Qwen3-0.6B → 4B gives +4 pp in-domain, +11 pp held-out); base/instruct/thinking checkpoints tie. Conclusion: "spending effort on the backbone and keeping the head minimal." — [arXiv:2611.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592)
- **[PREPRINT]** *From Large Language Models to Efficient System One Models via Adaptive Computation*, **Minh-Anh Nguyen**, arXiv:2611.16321 (**2026-11-12**): decision information concentrates in intermediate layers, "final six layers removable without loss"; jointly trains prefix sub-models with shared final layers/head and a lightweight gate. Efficiency comes from layer sharing/exits, not fast weights. — [arXiv:2611.16321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16321)
- **[VENDOR/SELF]** Laya 0.5 "Continuous learning": `from laya import Learner`: "answers from day one and learns from every answer your team confirms, on your own machine, in seconds on a CPU with no fine-tuning"; README explains `fit` "never touches the reader. It fits a small logistic head per question over the frozen features, a convex problem with one solution ... serves it only if it also beats the zero-shot engine on the same rows"; learns 300 answers in **34 s on 8 CPU threads** (0.72 s/answer, 6.4 GB RAM; GPU 40 ms, 2 GB) vs fine-tuning the encoder "89 s on a GPU with 9 GB and rewrites all 421M weights". This is **test-time adaptation of a small readout**, not fast-weight updating of the language model. — [Laya GitHub README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya/raw/main/README.md)
- **[SELF]** Laya 0.5 new engine "physics, not text generation": "Each candidate answer is a valley; the input is a particle that rolls and settles in one. Deterministic, constant cost, nothing generated", on top of a frozen reader (0.8B/4B/27B); outputs include conformal `prediction_set`, `abstain`, `unbound`, `route`. No fast weights claimed. — [Laya GitHub README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya)

### Inferences
- "Dynamic rule sets without retraining" is achievable in two different senses, often conflated: (a) **per-request schemas** — typed questions/criteria are part of the prompt, so new rules can be issued at call time (true for Jev and Laya, and also for constrained-decoding LLM baselines); (b) **online adaptation** — labels/teacher confirmations update small heads or calibration (Laya 0.5; Jev-DLC paper). Neither requires fast weights.
- The "one forward pass" claim is about *output sampling*: all options are rendered in the prompt and scored at one position/readout, avoiding autoregressive token generation. It does not mean one pass over the input with no prompt expansion, and it does not mean no training happened offline.
- Fast-weight TTT addresses long-context compression and online memory; Jev addresses decision-serving economics. Calling Jev a "fast-weight LLM" would be a category error unless future TypeSafe material says otherwise.

### Gaps
- No source shows TypeSafe describing internal weight updates at inference. No source describes Laya's vega/Learner as fast weights; its own docs explicitly describe logistic heads over frozen features.
- Whether Jev's "parallel sampler" or RLCD involves any meta-learned update rule is unknown; vendor FAQ on training method not accessible.

## Key Question 3: What is Laya, how does it relate to Jev, and what are its license, checkpoints, capabilities and documented limits?

### Takeaway
Laya is an **open-source Apache-2.0 non-autoregressive decision-model family** by **Nandakishor M / Convai Innovations**, explicitly framed as an open Jev-compatible alternative: same three typed primitives (`choice`, `score`, `noul`), same `POST /v1/systemone` wire format, but local weights and add-on learning/calibration. As of v0.5 (2026) it ships both classic ModernBERT/mmBERT encoders and a newer "vega" engine on a frozen LLM reader; documented limits are substantial and self-reported honestly.

### Cited Findings
- **[SELF/OSS]** GitHub `NandhaKishorM/laya`: "Non-autoregressive System 1 decision engine. Typed choice, score and yes/no decisions over any text in a single forward pass, in 100+ languages, with a router that picks the right checkpoint per request"; **Apache-2.0**; `pip install laya`; repo shows ~35.9k stars, ~3.2k forks at fetch time. Its `laya-serve` exposes "the same `POST /v1/systemone` request and response shape as TypeSafe Jev, so existing TypeSafe clients work by changing their base URL." Author's dev.to post title: "I built non-autoregressive decision models a year ago, then a frontier lab called it a..." (snippet). — [Laya GitHub](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya/raw/main/README.md)
- **[SELF]** Classic checkpoints (HF card table): `convaiinnovations/laya` — **ModernBERT-large, 421M params, 512 context**, English/guardrails/email triage; `convaiinnovations/laya-multilingual` — **mmBERT-base, 322M, 1024 default (up to 8192)**, 100+ languages; `convaiinnovations/laya-typed-decisions` — **ModernBERT-large, 421M, 1024 context**, four typed-decisions workflows. ### Inferences
- Laya is the best concrete implementation for a research project because it exposes weights, harnesses, failure modes and a self-auditing benchmark file; Jev does not. It is *not* a fast-weight model; use it to test the decision-model interface/hypothesis.
- Laya's own numbers change by engine version and checkpoint (classic encoders vs vega), and its self-reported head-to-heads often use *published Jev numbers measured by someone else, on different prompts/hardware* — the repo says so explicitly ("Jev figures are third-party published, never measured here ... treat them as indicative"). Do not pool these with independent paired evals.
- For a moderation demo, the most relevant self-reported warning is: held-out toxic-chat moderation **0.530** accuracy / macro-F1 0.400 ("barely above chance on a balanced split"; "hand-picked examples work, real traffic does not"), while email spam/phishing are ~0.993 but *in training*. — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)

### Gaps
- Laya v0.5 preprint not read in full (403 on landing page); exact algorithm for the "particle/valley" engine and whether it is a new optimizer vs metaphor needs primary-paper verification.
- No published independent replication of the v0.5 continuous-learning tables found; they are author-reported.
- Checkpoint download sizes in bytes not captured (parameter counts are); quantisation/ONAP runtime details not surveyed.

## Key Question 4: What do independent sources and benchmarks say about accuracy, latency, and calibration versus classical classifiers or LLM judges?

### Takeaway
Independent evidence supports the *existence* of the speed/interface advantage and partial calibration, but is **much less flattering than vendor claims**: Jev leads Laya on most agent decision points; Laya's own strong numbers depend on fine-tuning; both decision models fail on hard, fine-grained, or unfamiliar tasks; LLM judges with exact option probabilities remain a competitive and sometimes superior baseline. Latency comparisons are heavily hardware- and harness-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- **[PREPRINT/IND, broad]** Deußer, Ramamurthy, Sparrenberg & Sifa (arXiv:2609.37647, v2 2026-10-29) evaluated `jev-1.13.0` **zero-shot on 37 datasets**, one frozen template per dataset, full splits, **346,009 requests for under US$10**, comparing Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-E4B via exact option probabilities. Jev reaches **95–99%** on IMDB, SST-2, HellaSwag, ARC; beats Qwen on **27/37** datasets (no Qwen lead outside bootstrap intervals) and Gemma on **37/37**; "choice probabilities are well calibrated and support selective prediction". But "All three models degrade on low-resource languages, fine-grained or noisy labels, and rubric-based judgments"; binary probabilities **need tuned thresholds** — UNFAIR-ToS micro-F1 rises **0.50 → 0.75** after tuning. Sequential decisions: Jev completes **35%** unseen ALFWorld tasks, **42%** of 90 TextWorld games, **21%** BabyAI-Text, optimal Blackjack action **90%**, "often repeats actions that had no effect"; Qwen completes more ALFWorld (**54.5%**) and TextWorld (**51.1%**). Code, harness and raw responses released. — [arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)
- **[PREPRINT/IND, paired/self-audited]** **Jiawei Li**, *Fast Models, Slow Evidence: A Paired and Self-Audited Evaluation of System-1 Decision Models for LLM Agent Harnesses*, arXiv:2610.02267 (**2026-10-01**): **7,283 base cases + 6,640 robustness variants**, 11 decision points from 18 public sources, byte-identical inputs, paired tests, cross-hardware/day checks. "Jev is significantly more accurate on 9 of 11 decision points (**+10.8 to +46.0 pp**). Neither model beats chance on zero-shot model routing, and they tie on RAG relevance gating. Laya changes **30%** of its answers when the option order is reversed and degrades sharply with many or similar candidates (**31% at 50 nearest-neighbour tools, vs. 98% for Jev** on items with a unique correct tool)." Crucial self-audit: "Three analysis errors and one design confound distorted headline deployment claims: an omitted pre-screen cost (reported **23.9%** saving, actual **4.3%**), gate accuracy reported as end-to-end quality (**58% vs. 98%**), in-sample thresholds (**5%** target, up to **17%** held-out misses), and a 'channel effect' on injection false positives that vanishes with channel-native content." — [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)
- **[IND, GitHub]** Independent eval by **instax-dutta**, opened **2026-09-26**, Laya issue #555: 1,190 cases/1,550 typed questions/9 suites; evaluation split 952 cases/**1,240 decisions**; sealed SHA-256 manifest, seed 42, CPU-only, 4 CPUs/12 GB; Jev called over network. Overall accuracy **Laya 0.6863 / Jev 0.9065 / Qwen2.5-1.5B parallel-constrained-decoding 0.6048**. Jev ahead on all nine, significant on eight after Holm correction (deltas +0.057 to +0.550; triage +0.0573 not established, Holm p=0.0627). Choice-question ECE **0.0009–0.0028** for all three (close/unremarkable); "temperature scaling is not a dependable fix". p50/p95 per case: **Jev 314/387 ms, Laya 588/1,364 ms, Qwen PCD 3,948/13,471 ms**. Limitations stated by evaluators: labels human-reviewed-AI-assisted with one reviewer, no inter-annotator agreement/adjudication; default English Laya checkpoint used on all suites including multilingual (so multilingual row is not a property of `laya-multilingual`); Laya v0.3.11 weights. — [Laya issue #555](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/issues/555)
- **[SELF, conflicting with above]** Laya's own T4 numbers: p50 **32.8 ms** (multilingual, 1 question) / 39.5 ms (English), 103–332 questions/s batched; Jev "independently measured at 236–276 ms p50", hence "6–7× faster". - **[SEC, independent-ish]** Sebastian Raschka, *Language Models for Text Classification: From Bag-of-Words to Jev*, **2026-09-29**: "For a narrow, well-defined problem, Jev probably won't classify anything better, faster, or cheaper than a special-purpose classifier. No affiliation, no free access. — [Ahead of AI](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/classifier-history-and-jev)
- **[SELF]** Laya's own overall honest summary: "Where it loses. Zero-shot on generic public suites a large hosted model leads." And on prompt injection (ToxicChat, 1.8% attacks): "Jev untaught catches **79.1% at 0.3% refused** against **73.0% at 0.7%** for a fitted head." — [GitHub README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya)

### Inferences
- The robust finding is *shape*, not exact numbers: typed decision models buy large latency/cost reductions versus generating LLM judges, at some accuracy cost on difficult menus and with calibration that must be fitted/checked on own data. Classical fine-tuned classifiers remain competitive or better on narrow tasks, as Raschka shows and Laya's own README concedes.
- Because thresholding changes outcomes dramatically (0.50→0.75 F1; 5% target → 17% held-out misses), an honest moderation study must pre-register thresholds on a held-out calibration split and report both gate accuracy and end-to-end pipeline quality.
- Option order, menu size and near-duplicate labels are first-class threats to validity (30% answer flips; 31% at 50 tools); include permutation and cardinality ablations.

### Gaps
- No independent head-to-head found that compares Jev, Laya, a fine-tuned ModernBERT and an LLM judge *with identical prompts, thresholds, hardware and held-out moderation labels* — that is the gap a research project can fill.
- No peer-reviewed (conference/journal) independent benchmark; all 2026 evaluations are arXiv preprints or GitHub reports.
- Long-context/multimodal v0.5 claims have no independent benchmark yet.

## Key Question 5: Which claims are marketing, peer-reviewed, or reproducible evidence?

### Takeaway
Treat nearly all 2026 Jev/Laya performance headlines as **vendor/author self-reports or preprints**; only the older fast-weight/TTT papers are peer-reviewed, and they are unrelated to Jev. The strongest evidence for the *category* is independent arXiv work with released harnesses; the strongest evidence against inflated deployment claims is a self-audited evaluation that found its own headline savings were overstated by ~5×.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR, marketing]** "can't hallucinate", "two orders of magnitude faster and more efficient", "40x-200x faster", "$0.042/MTok", "0% type errors" ( admitted "not empirical"), "off the charts ... Pareto frontier": all from TypeSafe's own launch post with its own nuance section. — [TypeSafe AI](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- **[VENDOR, marketing secondary]** LangChain blog reports "up to 200x faster inference and 400x lower cost than comparable LLMs on [tasks]" — a restatement of company claims, not independent measurement. — [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-a-harness-with-jev)
- **[SELF, reproducible-in-principle]** Laya publishes BENCHMARKS.md, result JSON paths, scripts and explicit non-reproduction of some published columns (e.g. multilingual sweep changed under three machines; issue #208), which is stronger practice than vendor claims but still author-run. — [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)
- **[PREPRINT, independent, code released]** Deußer et al. release code, harness and raw responses; Rastogi re-implements readouts under one protocol; Li releases cases, raw outputs and analysis code and audits their own errors. These are the citable evidence base, but **not peer-reviewed**. — [2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647); [2611.15592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15592); [2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)
- **[PEER]** Only peer-reviewed items in this brief are the fast-weight/TTL lineage: Schlag/Irie/Schmidhuber (ICML 2021) and Hu et al. (ICML 2025); neither is evidence about Jev. — [2102.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174); [2505.20633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20633)
- **[PREPRINT, author-claimed]** "RLCD" is named by TypeSafe and used by Laya ("trained with reinforcement learning against strictly proper scoring rules (RLCD)"), but no detailed public derivation/ablation of TypeSafe's RLCD was found; Laya's preprint is a Preprints.org posting. — [TypeSafe](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev); [HF Laya card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya/raw/main/README.md)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** "Jev trained exclusively on synthetic data" (TechCrunch snippet), "quarter of Fortune 500 onboarded" (CEO via Understanding AI), "Jev 1.14 cannot be taught, only prompted" (Laya README). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers); [Understanding AI](https://www.understandingai.org/p/understanding-jev-the-new-model-everyone); [Laya README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya)

### Inferences
- Evidence hierarchy for a paper: (1) own paired, pre-registered eval with public harness; (2) independent arXiv eval with released raw outputs; (3) author-run repo benchmarks with fixed seeds; (4) vendor blog/demo; (5) press/blog repetition.
- Avoid writing "Jev is calibrated / doesn't hallucinate / 200× faster" as fact. Write "TypeSafe claims X; independent evaluation of jev-1.13.0 found Y under protocol Z".
- The user's "dynamic rule sets without retraining" is best framed as a *testable hypothesis about prompt-specified schemas and cheap local adaptation*, not as an established property of fast-weight models.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis or systematic review found; no type-safe decision model benchmark registered as a conference/workshop challenge.

## Key Question 6: What is substantiated vs unsupported for turning a moderation demo into an honest research project?

### Takeaway
Substantiated: the interface (typed one-pass decisions), large latency/cost gains vs generative judges, and the feasibility of open local replicas. Unsupported: Jev-as-fast-weights, architecture/training-data claims, "cannot hallucinate", vendor speedup multiples as general results, and Laya-vs-Jev win claims from non-comparable harnesses.

### Cited Findings
- Substantiated by independent runnable work: typed decision models score fixed options in one pass; Jev 95–99% on easy classification sets; calibrated choice probabilities *on some suites*; threshold tuning matters; hard/rubric/multilingual/sequential cases remain weak. — [arXiv:2609.37647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)
- Substantiated by paired tests: Jev > Laya on 9/11 agent decision points; Laya option-order instability; neither beats chance on zero-shot model routing; self-audited deployment savings can be ~5× smaller than headline. — [arXiv:2610.02267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)
- Substantiated by Laya's own docs: local Apache-2.0 weights, 421M/322M classic checkpoints, 0.8B–27B vega readers, 73k context as of v0.5, conformal sets/abstain, small-head learning on frozen features, honest failure documentation (77-label ceiling, unbound false-positive rate, held-out moderation 0.530). — [Laya GitHub](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya); [BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md)
- Unsubstantiated/marketing: "frontier intelligence", "cannot hallucinate", "0% type errors" as an empirical result, "200×/400×" as a general result, "trained exclusively on synthetic data", "Jev's architecture" as a public technical object. — [TypeSafe](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers)

### Inferences
- Recommended research framing: *"Typed one-pass decision models as a latency/calibration trade-off for moderation: Jev API vs Apache-2.0 Laya vs fine-tuned encoder vs constrained LLM judge, under identical prompts, thresholds, and held-out community-moderation labels."*
- Minimum honest design implied by the evidence: (i) sealed/frozen test set with adjudicated labels and disagreement metrics; (ii) calibration split separate from test, pre-registered routing thresholds; (iii) report macro-F1 + ECE/Brier + coverage/precision at auto-route, plus p50/p95 latency and $/1k decisions; (iv) option-order permutation and 5/20/50-label cardinality sweeps; (v) out-of-distribution and prompt-injection probes; (vi) comparison to a fine-tuned small encoder baseline, since that is the strongest cheap alternative.
- Do not cite Laya's 96.0% "continuous learning" or 0.766 typed-decisions as general capability: both are author-run, template-specific, and the latter depends entirely on fine-tuning.

### Gaps
- A moderation-specific independent dataset with real traffic and dual annotation was not found; ToxicChat is the closest and appears only in author self-reports.
- Cost-per-decision figures for Laya self-hosting under a fixed SLO exist in the repo (`research/results/nvidia_capacity_20260925.json`) but were not analysed here.
- Whether Jev 1.14/1.13 changed architecture vs prompt-only rules is unknown; versioned public changelog not located.
